## 1. ESTABLISHED COSMOLOGICAL KNOWLEDGE

The following topics have extensive observational
and theoretical support:

- The universe is expanding.
- The cosmic microwave background exists.
- Matter is distributed non-uniformly at large scales.
- Gravity influences the growth of cosmic structures.
- The observable universe has a finite age
  within standard cosmological models.
- The Lambda-CDM model provides a successful
  description of many cosmological observations.

These statements do not imply that every aspect
of the universe is understood.

---

## 2. MODEL ASSUMPTIONS

The current reference model assumes:

- A homogeneous and isotropic background.
- General relativity at cosmological scales.
- A cosmological constant.
- Cold dark matter.
- A specified cosmological parameter set.
- A defined mathematical description of expansion.

These are model assumptions and approximations.
They must not be confused with direct observations.

---

## 3. UNRESOLVED QUESTIONS

The current project does not establish:

- The ultimate origin of the universe.
- What preceded the hot Big Bang, if anything.
- The physical nature of dark matter.
- The physical nature of dark energy.
- The origin of primordial density perturbations.
- Whether the observable universe represents
  the entirety of reality.
- Whether alternative cosmological descriptions
  provide a better fundamental explanation.

These questions remain outside the capabilities
of the current simulation.

---

## 4. COMPUTATIONAL LIMITATIONS

A numerical simulation cannot reproduce every
physical scale of the real universe.

Its behavior depends on:

- Spatial resolution.
- Time resolution.
- Numerical precision.
- Available computational resources.
- Implemented physical equations.
- Initial conditions.

Unresolved scales may require approximations.

Every approximation must be documented.

---

## 5. EXPERIMENTAL CONSEQUENCE

No unknown physical property may be silently
replaced by an arbitrary value.

If a parameter must be assumed for computational
reasons, its value and justification must be recorded.

If a question cannot be represented by the model,
it must remain explicitly outside the experiment.
