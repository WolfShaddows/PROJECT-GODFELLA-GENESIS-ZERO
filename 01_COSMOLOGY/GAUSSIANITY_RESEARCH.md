## 2. Gaussian primordial perturbations

Gaussianity describes the statistical properties
of the primordial perturbation field.

For a Gaussian random field, its statistical
distribution is determined by its mean and
two-point correlation function.

The power spectrum characterizes the
two-point statistics.

Gaussianity does not imply spatial uniformity,
absence of fluctuations, or absence of later
structure formation.

## 3. Primordial non-Gaussianity

Non-Gaussianity refers to deviations from
Gaussian statistical behavior.

The bispectrum is one statistical observable
used to investigate three-point correlations.

The parameter f_NL is used to characterize
amplitudes of particular non-Gaussian templates.

Its interpretation depends on the template
and convention.

Common configurations include:

- Local
- Equilateral
- Orthogonal

These configurations are not interchangeable.

## 4. Planck 2018 observational results

Combined temperature and polarization analysis:

Local:
f_NL = -0.9 ± 5.1

Equilateral:
f_NL = -26 ± 47

Orthogonal:
f_NL = -38 ± 24

Confidence level:
68% statistical.

The results show no statistically significant
detection of primordial non-Gaussianity in
these standard configurations.

They are compatible with Gaussian primordial
scalar perturbations.

This does not establish that all possible
forms of non-Gaussianity are exactly zero.

## 5. Isocurvature constraints

Planck 2018 Results X investigated correlated
mixed adiabatic and isocurvature models.

95% confidence upper limits on the non-adiabatic
contribution to observed CMB temperature variance:

CDM isocurvature:
1.3%

Neutrino density isocurvature:
1.7%

Neutrino velocity isocurvature:
1.7%

These limits are model-dependent constraints.
They are not direct measurements of actual
isocurvature fractions in every physical context.

## 6. Implications for the experiment

The observational evidence is compatible with
approximately Gaussian and predominantly
adiabatic primordial scalar perturbations.

However:

- Gaussianity does not specify a spatial realization.
- A power spectrum does not specify Fourier phases.
- A statistical constraint does not uniquely
  determine an individual universe realization.
- Non-Gaussianity is constrained, not universally
  excluded.
- Isocurvature limits depend on the tested models.

## 7. Open research questions

- Investigate the precise statistical definition
  of Gaussian random fields.
- Investigate Fourier phase distributions.
- Investigate higher-order correlation functions.
- Investigate the dependence of f_NL on conventions.
- Investigate additional non-Gaussian templates.
- Investigate adiabatic and isocurvature initial modes.
- Investigate how these constraints transfer into
  a three-dimensional matter perturbation field.
