# GENESIS ZERO — EMERGENT COMPUTATION
## Computational Substrate Research

Project: PROJECT GODFELLA

Status: SCIENTIFIC RESEARCH
Implementation: NOT STARTED
NEW ENGINE: NOT APPROVED
AI PARTICIPANTS: NONE

---

## 1. Central research question

Can information-processing capabilities arise
spontaneously through the dynamics and interactions
of a computational substrate without introducing
pre-existing artificial intelligence?

No emergence is assumed.

Absence of emergence is an acceptable result.

---

## 2. Research direction A — Cellular automata

Cellular automata provide discrete systems
whose states evolve through local transition rules.

Research reference:

Lizier et al. (2012).

Local measures of information storage
in complex distributed computation.

Information Sciences, 208, 39-54.

DOI: 10.1016/j.ins.2012.04.016

Research questions:

- Can persistent structures appear?
- Can local structures retain information?
- Can information propagate through interactions?
- Can computational behavior arise without
  explicitly encoding a particular computation?

Important limitation:

A cellular automaton already contains
a predefined transition rule.

Computational universality does not imply
spontaneous intelligence.

No cellular automaton rule has been selected.

---

## 3. Research direction B — Physical computation

Physical and unconventional computing investigate
how the dynamics of physical substrates can
perform information processing.

Research references:

A photonics perspective on computing
with physical substrates (2024).

Reviews in Physics, 12, 100093.

DOI: 10.1016/j.revip.2024.100093

Computing with oscillators from theoretical
underpinnings to applications and demonstrators.

npj Unconventional Computing (2024).

Research questions:

- Can information be represented by physical states?
- Can interactions transform information?
- Can a substrate retain information over time?
- What properties are necessary for computation?
- Is an explicit conventional processor necessary?

Important limitation:

A physical substrate capable of computation
does not necessarily develop intelligence.

No physical substrate has been selected.

---

## 4. Research direction C — Open-ended evolution

Research reference:

The MODES Toolbox (2019).

Measurements of Open-Ended Dynamics
in Evolving Systems.

DOI: 10.1162/artl_a_00288

Research questions:

- How can novelty be measured?
- How can persistent diversity be distinguished
  from random fluctuations?
- Can new structures continue appearing?
- Can system organization change over time?

Important limitation:

Open-ended evolution is not equivalent
to intelligence or consciousness.

No evolutionary mechanism has been selected.

---

## 5. Proposed observation categories

CATEGORY 1 — SELF-ORGANIZATION

Identify structures that persist beyond
ordinary transient fluctuations.

CATEGORY 2 — INFORMATION STORAGE

Determine whether past states contain measurable
information about future states.

CATEGORY 3 — INFORMATION TRANSMISSION

Determine whether information propagates
through local interactions.

CATEGORY 4 — INFORMATION TRANSFORMATION

Investigate whether interactions transform
information in reproducible ways.

CATEGORY 5 — ADAPTIVE BEHAVIOR

Investigate whether responses depend on
retained information from previous experiences.

These categories are research targets,
not a mandatory evolutionary sequence.

---

## 6. Experimental neutrality

The next experiment must not introduce:

- Pretrained AI models.
- Intelligent agents.
- Language models.
- Predefined intelligence objectives.
- Reward functions targeting intelligence.
- Manually encoded solutions.
- Human intervention during execution.

The external research team remains isolated
from the experimental environment.

No observations may be fed back into the system.

---

## 7. Measurement requirements

Before implementation, define:

- Operational definition of information storage.
- Operational definition of information transmission.
- Operational definition of computation.
- Persistence criteria.
- Noise baselines.
- Randomized null models.
- Reproducibility requirements.
- Independent validation procedures.

Visual complexity is insufficient evidence.

Statistical fluctuations must not be confused
with persistent information processing.

## 10. Fundamental principle

We do not introduce intelligence.

We do not program an intelligent destination.

We do not select outcomes because they appear complex.

We investigate what the substrate can do
under its own defined dynamics.

We observe what happens.
