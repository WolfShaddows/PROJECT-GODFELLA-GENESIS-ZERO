# GENESIS ZERO — DUAL UNIVERSE EXPERIMENT

Project: PROJECT GODFELLA

Status: EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN
Execution: NOT STARTED
Official launch: NOT AUTHORIZED

---

## 1. Fundamental architecture

Two independent computational universes will evolve
in parallel.

Neither universe will communicate with, influence,
control or modify the other.

External researchers remain observers.

The purpose is to investigate what happens under
two different environmental conditions.

No outcome is predetermined.

---

## 2. UNIVERSE A — UNMODIFIED EVOLUTION

Purpose:

Observe the evolution of a system without introducing
additional components after its initialization.

Conditions:

- Independently initialized state.
- Independently executed evolution.
- No external introductions during execution.
- No intelligent agents.
- No predefined evolutionary objective.
- No human intervention after launch.
- External observation only.

---

## 3. UNIVERSE B — EVOLUTION WITH INTRODUCTIONS

Purpose:

Observe the evolution of an independent system
that receives predefined external introductions.

Conditions:

- Independently initialized state.
- Independently executed evolution.
- External introductions defined before launch.
- No intelligent agents.
- No predefined evolutionary objective.
- No human intervention after launch.
- External observation only.

The nature, magnitude, timing and frequency
of introductions remain undecided.

They must be documented before execution.

---

## 4. PARALLEL INDEPENDENCE

The following conditions are mandatory:

- Universe A cannot access Universe B.
- Universe B cannot access Universe A.
- No shared evolving state.
- No shared random generator state.
- No exchange of information.
- No adaptive parameter exchange.
- No feedback between experiments.
- No human intervention based on observations.
- No cross-contamination of experimental data.

The experiments may share an external observation
and evidence-storage infrastructure, provided
that it cannot influence either simulation.

---

## 5. COMPARABILITY

The experimental design must document:

- Common model assumptions.
- Initialization methodology.
- Independent random seeds.
- Computational rules.
- Execution duration.
- Observation frequency.
- Measurement methodology.

Differences between A and B must be explicitly
identified before launch.

The experiments must not be modified to produce
similar outcomes.

---

## 6. EXTERNAL OBSERVATION

Researchers may observe and record:

- State evolution.
- Persistent structures.
- Spatial organization.
- Interactions.
- Information transmission.
- Information storage.
- Statistical changes.
- Unexpected behaviors.

Observation must not create a feedback channel
into either universe.

Visual complexity alone is not evidence
of intelligence or consciousness.

---

## 7. SCIENTIFIC INTEGRITY

Both experiments must preserve:

- Original configurations.
- Execution logs.
- Raw observations.
- Checkpoints.
- Reproducibility information.
- Cryptographic evidence hashes.

Negative results must be preserved.

Unexpected results must not be selectively discarded.

No intelligence emergence is assumed.

---

## 8. UNRESOLVED DESIGN DECISIONS

Before implementation, researchers must establish:

1. The computational substrate.
2. The local interaction rules.
3. The initial-state distribution.
4. The nature of Universe B introductions.
5. The observation and measurement protocol.
6. The execution architecture.
7. The isolation and interference controls.

No official execution is permitted until
these decisions have been documented and audited.

---

## 9. EXPERIMENTAL PRINCIPLE

Two universes.

Two independent evolutions.

Two separate histories.

One external research team.

No communication between universes.

No predetermined destination.

We observe what happens.
