# THE FIRST STATE — FOUNDATIONAL RESEARCH

Project: PROJECT GODFELLA
Experiment: GENESIS ZERO

Status: OPEN RESEARCH — NO INITIAL MODEL APPROVED
Execution: NOT STARTED

## 1. Research objective

Investigate what is minimally required for an abstract
computational state to exist and undergo change.

This research does not attempt to establish the absolute
origin of physical reality.

It does not assume that the computational requirements
of a simulator correspond to fundamental properties
of the physical universe.

## 2. Fundamental distinction

Two domains must remain separate.

### Computational existence

The formal requirements used by a computer program
to represent and process a model.

Examples that may require investigation:

- A representation of possible states.
- A particular initial state.
- A transition mechanism.
- A computational ordering of operations.
- A representation of information.

These are modeling concepts, not automatically
physical properties of the modeled universe.

### Physical existence

The properties that the experimental model claims
to represent as physical.

Their nature and relationship remain open questions.

No equivalence between computational and physical
existence is assumed.

## 3. Research questions

### 3.1 Space

- Must the initial state contain spatial structure?
- Could spatial relations emerge from something more basic?
- Would choosing a grid impose an artificial geometry?
- What assumptions are introduced by choosing
  one, two, or three spatial dimensions?

Decision:
OPEN — NO SPATIAL REPRESENTATION APPROVED.

### 3.2 Time

- Must time exist in the initial state?
- Is a computational step equivalent to physical time?
- Could temporal order emerge from state transitions?
- Can a model describe change without assuming
  a pre-existing physical clock?

Decision:
OPEN — NO PHYSICAL TIME MODEL APPROVED.

### 3.3 Matter and entities

- Must the initial state contain identifiable entities?
- Can entities emerge from relations or interactions?
- Does defining particles impose unnecessary assumptions?
- What distinguishes a physical entity from a
  computational representation?

Decision:
OPEN — NO PARTICLE OR ENTITY CONTENT APPROVED.

### 3.4 Energy

- Must energy be defined in the initial state?
- Under what conditions is energy conservation applicable?
- Does an abstract computational state require
  a physical energy interpretation?

Decision:
OPEN — NO ENERGY MODEL APPROVED.

### 3.5 Change and evolution

- What makes a state capable of changing?
- Must a transition rule exist from the beginning?
- Could the transition mechanism itself be part
  of the model's assumptions?
- What distinguishes deterministic from stochastic
  evolution?
- What does an unchanged state mean?

Decision:
OPEN — NO TRANSITION LAW APPROVED.

## 4. Formal computational framework

A general mathematical model of an evolving system
can be represented using:

X: a set of possible states.

x0: a particular initial state.

F: a transition function, if deterministic evolution
is assumed.

Alternatively, a stochastic transition mechanism
could be considered.

This notation is only a formal research framework.

It does not establish that physical reality is
discrete, deterministic, stochastic, or computational.

No particular framework is selected for GENESIS ZERO.

## 5. Research boundaries

The following assumptions are not approved:

- A pre-existing three-dimensional space.
- A pre-existing physical clock.
- A predefined particle inventory.
- A predefined temperature.
- A predefined density.
- A predefined energy distribution.
- A predefined physical origin.
- A predefined evolutionary destination.

No unknown physical phenomenon will be silently
replaced by an invented physical law.

Hypothetical models must be explicitly identified.

## 6. Neutrality requirements

No life, intelligence, organisms, civilizations,
or technology will be inserted.

No artificial evolutionary direction will be imposed.

No initial configuration will be selected because
it is expected to produce a desired outcome.

Unexpected results and the absence of emergence
must both be preserved.
