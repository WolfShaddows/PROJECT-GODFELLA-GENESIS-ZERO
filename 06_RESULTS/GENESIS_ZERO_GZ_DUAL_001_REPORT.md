## 1. Objective

Compare two independently executed computational
systems evolving from an identical initial state.

Universe A evolves without external introductions.

Universe B receives a predefined localized
mathematical perturbation.

The purpose is to observe differences in their
evolution under these conditions.

## 6. Scientific limitations

The experiment uses an abstract computational
scalar field with local diffusion and additive noise.

The perturbation is a mathematical field increment.
It is not a physical energy injection.

The random generators use independent seeds.
Consequently, differences between final states
cannot be attributed exclusively to the perturbation.

The model does not contain explicit mechanisms
for memory, symbolic processing, learning,
adaptation or agency.

No spontaneous intelligence emergence was tested
or demonstrated.

This experiment does not represent a complete
physical universe.

## 8. Important implementation note

The worker summary currently evaluates its field
statistics for the "initial" entry after evolution
has completed. Therefore, that particular summary
entry must not be interpreted as the initial state.

Initial measurements must be obtained from
the first row of each statistics.csv file
or from the preserved initial-state arrays.

This limitation does not invalidate the recorded
final measurements or the raw observation records.

## 9. Conclusion

GZ-DUAL-001 successfully executed two independent
computational evolutions from identical initial states.

A measurable difference was observed between
their final states.

The experiment demonstrates the behavior of this
particular mathematical model under the specified
conditions.

It does not establish spontaneous emergence
of information processing or intelligence.
