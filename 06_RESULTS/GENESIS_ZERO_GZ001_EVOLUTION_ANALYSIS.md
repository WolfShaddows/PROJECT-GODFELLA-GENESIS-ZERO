## 1. Objective

Study the evolution of the numerical field
throughout the 500 cycles of GZ-001.

Three statistical measurements were examined:

- Field variance.
- Field mean.
- Shannon entropy of the value distribution.

## 2. Data source

The analysis was generated by reproducing
the original GZ-001 configuration:

Grid: 128 x 128
Cells: 16,384
Evolution steps: 500
Observations: 501
Random seed: 20261101

The original engine and its transition rules
were not modified.

Therefore, exact conservation of the total
field value is not expected.

## 5. Shannon entropy of value distribution

Method:

Histogram-based Shannon entropy.

Histogram bins: 64

Shared histogram boundaries were used
across all observations.

Initial entropy:

5.997218812635807 bits

Final entropy:

3.0432262519421496 bits

The reduction indicates that the distribution
of numerical values became more concentrated.

This statistic measures the distribution
of field values, not spatial organization,
thermodynamic entropy, or total system complexity.

## 6. Observed behavior

The three measurements show:

- Strong initial reduction in variance.
- Subsequent slowing of homogenization.
- Small fluctuations and drift in the mean.
- Reduction in histogram-based value entropy.
- Persistent nonzero fluctuations at cycle 500.

These observations are consistent with
the implemented diffusion-plus-noise dynamics.

## 7. Scientific limitations

The observed behavior belongs to this particular
abstract numerical model.

It does not establish:

- Physical cosmological evolution.
- Thermodynamic entropy production.
- Emergent life.
- Intelligence or consciousness.
- General principles of universal evolution.

The diffusion rule explicitly favors local
homogenization.

The stochastic term continuously introduces
fluctuations.

## 8. Open question

Does the observed variance plateau represent
a statistically stationary regime?

This has not yet been established.

A longer observation period and quantitative
stationarity tests are required.
